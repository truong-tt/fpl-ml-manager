# Fantasy Premier League (FPL) 101

This document explains the rules and mechanics of the official Fantasy Premier League game. If you are reviewing the Machine Learning architecture in this repository and are unfamiliar with soccer or fantasy sports, this guide provides the domain context you need before the system design will make full sense.

---

## What is FPL?

Fantasy Premier League is a game where you act as the manager of a virtual soccer team composed of real-life players from the English Premier League (EPL). Each week, your virtual players score points based on their actual performances in real EPL matches — goals, assists, clean sheets, and more. The English Premier League season runs for 38 rounds of matches, called **Gameweeks**, and the goal is to accumulate more total points than every other manager by the end of Gameweek 38.

The reason this is a genuinely hard optimization problem — and not just a trivia game — is that it combines a constrained budget, positional quotas, team limits, transfer penalties, and high-variance match outcomes all at once. No single player is obviously correct; every decision involves a tradeoff.

---

## 1. Squad Selection and Budget Constraints

At the start of the season, you build a squad of exactly **15 players** subject to three hard constraints.

The first is **budget**: you have a virtual budget of £100.0 million. Player prices range from around £4.0m for fringe players to £15.0m for elite superstars, and you must field a complete 15-man squad without exceeding that ceiling.

The second is **positional composition**: your squad must contain exactly 2 Goalkeepers (GK), 5 Defenders (DEF), 5 Midfielders (MID), and 3 Forwards (FWD). You cannot substitute one position for another — the counts are fixed.

The third is a **club cap**: you may select at most 3 players from any single real-world Premier League club. This prevents managers from simply loading up on the best team in the league.

These three constraints together define what the ML system models as a bounded knapsack problem — you are trying to maximize expected points subject to a budget, fixed cardinality per category, and entity limits.

---

## 2. The Starting XI and Substitutes

Before each Gameweek's transfer deadline, you must nominate **11 starters** from your 15-man squad. Only those 11 players contribute points to your score; the remaining 4 sit on your bench and score nothing unless an auto-substitution is triggered.

Your starting 11 must respect a valid formation, which means at minimum 1 Goalkeeper, 3 Defenders, and 1 Forward. You can play any combination beyond that (e.g. 4–4–2, 3–5–2, 4–3–3), giving you some tactical flexibility in how you weight your attacking versus defensive options.

**Auto-substitutions** exist to protect you from complete blanks. If one of your 11 starters plays zero minutes in real life — due to injury, suspension, or being an unused substitute — the game will automatically swap them with the highest-priority bench player who *did* play, provided the swap does not break the valid formation rules. This is why it matters that your bench players are reasonable rather than pure filler: an auto-sub from the bench is the only way to recover points when a starter is unexpectedly absent.

---

## 3. Captaincy

Each Gameweek you must designate one player as your **Captain** and one as your **Vice-Captain**. The Captain's points total is doubled for that Gameweek, making this decision the single highest-leverage choice you make each week. Picking the right captain on a player who then scores a hat-trick can swing 20+ points relative to the average manager.

If your Captain plays zero minutes, the double automatically transfers to your Vice-Captain instead — so the Vice-Captain choice is a meaningful risk-management decision, not just an afterthought.

## 4. How Players Score Points

The scoring system is the core of what the ML pipeline is trying to model and predict. Points are awarded per player based on real-world match events, and the values are heavily position-dependent.

### Playing Time

Every player earns **1 point** simply for taking the field for up to 60 minutes, and **2 points** for playing 60 minutes or more. This distinction matters significantly in the ML system — a player who is consistently substituted off at the 55-minute mark earns half the appearance points of a player who regularly completes 90 minutes, before any goals or assists are even considered.

### Attacking Returns

Goals are the highest-value single event in FPL, but the reward decreases as you move further up the pitch. A Goalkeeper scoring a goal earns **10 points**, a Defender earns **6 points**, a Midfielder earns **5 points**, and a Forward earns **4 points**. The rationale is that a goalkeeper scoring is an extraordinary event and should be rewarded accordingly, while forward goals are more expected and therefore worth less per occurrence.

Assists earn **3 points** for all positions. As of the 2025/26 season, the assist rules have been broadened to award credit in more situations, including deflections off defenders, rebounds off the woodwork, and inadvertent touches before a teammate scores.

### Defensive Returns

Clean sheets — meaning the player's team conceded no goals while they were on the pitch — reward Goalkeepers and Defenders with **4 points** and Midfielders with **1 point**. Forwards receive nothing for a clean sheet. To qualify, the player must have been on the pitch for at least 60 minutes.

Under the 2026/27 rules, outfield players continue to earn a **defensive contribution bonus of +2 points** for reaching position-specific thresholds in a single match. Defenders need 10 combined Clearances, Blocks, Interceptions, and Tackles (CBIT). Midfielders and Forwards need 12 combined Clearances, Blocks, Interceptions, Tackles, and ball Recoveries (CBIRT). The award is capped at two points per match.

Goalkeepers and Defenders also lose **1 point for every 2 goals conceded** by their team while they are on the pitch — a direct incentive to select players from defensively solid clubs.

### Negative Points

Several in-match events incur point deductions: a yellow card costs **–1 point**, a red card costs **–3 points**, a missed penalty costs **–2 points**, and an own goal costs **–2 points**. These are not trivial — a player who scores and then receives a red card ends the match on net 2 points (5 for the goal, –3 for the red), which is a significant drag on your score.

### The Bonus Points System (BPS)

At the end of every match, the three best-performing players on either side — determined by the Bonus Points System — are awarded **3 points** to the top performer, **2 to the second**, and **1 to the third**. For 2026/27, being tackled no longer costs BPS, clearances/blocks/interceptions earn 1 BPS per three actions rather than per two, and goalkeepers receive 2 BPS for any save plus 1 for an inside-box save and 1 for a big-chance save. A penalty save is worth 7 BPS before the separate big-chance-save point.

Official scores are finalized at 09:00 UK time on the day after the Gameweek's final match. This project therefore treats `data_checked`, not the final whistle alone, as the boundary for training and season replay.

## 5. Transfers and Chips

Because player form, injuries, and fixture difficulty change week to week, you must continuously manage your squad through the transfer market.

### Free Transfers and Point Penalties

You receive **1 Free Transfer** per Gameweek. If you do not use it, it rolls over and you bank it for the following week — but you can accumulate no more than **5 banked free transfers** at any time. The one-off December transfer top-up used for AFCON in 2025/26 does not apply in 2026/27.

If you want to make more transfers than your free allocation in a given week, each additional transfer costs **–4 points**, a penalty deducted directly from your score. These are called "hits." The optimizer in this repository models this penalty explicitly: a transfer is only worth making if the expected gain in points from the incoming player strictly exceeds the expected points from the outgoing player *plus* the 4-point cost.

### Player Price Changes

Player prices shift by £0.1m increments based on real-world transfer activity among all FPL managers. A player being bought heavily by the community will rise in price; one being sold heavily will fall. This creates a secondary objective of maximizing your team's total value over the season — selling a player whose price has risen before it falls back generates "profit" that can be reinvested into more expensive options.

### Chips

Chips are one-time-use power-ups that temporarily override the normal rules for a single Gameweek. Under the unchanged 2026/27 structure, managers receive **two sets of chips** per season — one set for the first half of the season and a refreshed set for the second half. Each set contains the same four chips.

The **Wildcard** grants unlimited free transfers for one Gameweek, allowing a complete squad overhaul at no points cost. The **Free Hit** also allows unlimited free transfers, but only for a single week — your squad reverts to its previous state at the next deadline, making it best deployed around fixture "blanks" where many teams do not play. The **Triple Captain** triples your captain's points rather than doubling them, making it a high-reward chip to hold for a Gameweek where an elite player faces an easy fixture. The **Bench Boost** adds the points of your four benched players to your Gameweek total, rewarding managers who have invested in quality depth.

An important rule: the first set of chips expires at the Gameweek 19 deadline — any unused chips from the first half are forfeited. Additionally, playing a Wildcard or Free Hit no longer resets your banked free transfers, which is a change from previous seasons worth keeping in mind when planning transfer strategy.

> **Note:** The production lineup includes chip recommendations, while the season replay simulates both chip sets with bounded heuristic trigger windows. These are decision heuristics rather than separately trained chip models.
